FSSAI licence tier finder and renewal tracker for food businesses

A free, single-binary tool that works out which of the three FSSAI tiers your food business needs and tells you exactly which rule put you there — then keeps every licence expiry date in front of you before it lapses.

Food manufacturers, traders, restaurants and home kitchens FSSAI · FSS Act 2006 · FoSCoS

What it does

Three questions trip up nearly every new food business in India: which FSSAI authorisation do I actually need, what do I have to carry to get it, and when does it run out. This tool answers all three on your own machine.

You answer a short questionnaire — turnover, what the business does, how much food or milk it handles a day, how many states it operates in, and whether it imports, exports, sells through e-commerce or sits inside an airport or seaport. The tool returns:

  • the tier: Basic Registration, State Licence or Central Licence;
  • the reasoning chain — every threshold that fired and every one that cleared, in the order the rules were applied;
  • the document checklist for that tier and that kind of business, exportable as CSV;
  • the indicative government fee for each validity from one to five years;
  • a renewal watchdog over the licences you save, sorted by urgency, with alerts at ninety, thirty and seven days and an overdue state after that.

The reasoning chain is the part that matters. Being told "State Licence" is not useful on its own; being told "milk handled 3,000 litres a day falls in the 500 to 50,000 litres a day band, which is a State Licence" is something you can check, argue with, and act on.

Who it's for

Food manufacturers, processors, traders, wholesalers, retailers, restaurants, caterers, transporters, cold stores, home kitchens and cloud kitchens anywhere in India — the small operators who do not have a consultant on retainer and who most often apply for the wrong tier, or let a licence lapse because nobody was watching the date.

It is equally useful to the person who already holds a licence: if turnover has grown, a second state has opened, or exports have started, the tier may have changed underneath you.

The rule it implements

The three tiers are set by the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 and the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations, 2011. Applications are filed on FoSCoS, the Food Safety Compliance System operated by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India. A Basic Registration is applied for in Form A; a State or Central Licence in Form B.

The tool applies three rules, in this order.

  1. Central Licence if any one of these is true: annual turnover above ₹20 crore; the business imports food; it exports food or is a 100% export oriented unit; it is an e-commerce food business operator; it operates in two or more states, counted from the head office; its premises are inside an airport or seaport; or its production capacity is above what a State Licence covers.
  2. Basic Registration if none of those apply and the business is a petty food business operator: turnover at or below ₹12 lakh, food capacity at or below 100 kg a day, and milk at or below 500 litres a day.
  3. State Licence for everything in between — turnover above ₹12 lakh and up to ₹20 crore, with capacity inside the State ceilings.

The capacity ceilings that separate a State Licence from a Central Licence are 2 metric tonnes a day of food manufacturing or processing, and 50,000 litres a day of milk — or 2,500 metric tonnes of milk solids a year. Milk between 500 and 50,000 litres a day sits in the State Licence band, which is why a small dairy can need a State Licence on a turnover that would otherwise be petty.

Install & run

One binary, no dependencies, nothing to install alongside it. With Go 1.25 or later:

# build it once, then run the binary anywhere
go build -o fssai-license-navigator .
./fssai-license-navigator

# or run it straight from the source directory
go run .

# choose a different port or data file
./fssai-license-navigator -addr 127.0.0.1:8137 -data ~/licences.json

Then open http://127.0.0.1:8080/. The user interface is embedded in the binary, so the one file is the whole tool — you can copy it onto a machine with no internet connection and it will work exactly the same.

What it stores

One local JSON file, ./fssai-license-navigator-data.json by default, holding the licences you saved and any threshold or fee you corrected. The path is printed when the tool starts and shown in the interface.

Nothing else. The tool binds to 127.0.0.1 so it is not reachable from your network, it makes no outbound network calls of any kind, there is no account, no telemetry, no analytics and no third-party code or fonts. The data file is written through a temporary file and renamed into place, so an interrupted write cannot corrupt what you had. Back it up by copying that one file.

Features

Tier questionnaire

Turnover, activity, capacity, milk, states and the five facts that override everything else — recalculated as you type.

The reasoning chain

Every rule that fired and every rule that cleared, quoting the figure it was compared against.

Document checklist

The documents for your tier and your kind of business, with who each one is asked of and what satisfies it.

CSV export

Take the checklist to a spreadsheet, a printer or whoever is collecting the papers for you.

Indicative fee

The government fee for the tier and category, priced for each validity from one to five years.

Renewal watchdog

Every saved licence sorted by urgency, with alerts at ninety, thirty and seven days, and the accrued late fee once one has lapsed.

Figures you can correct

Every threshold, ceiling and fee is editable and saved, so a revision by the authority does not make the tool wrong.

Entirely offline

Loopback only, one local file, no network calls, no dependencies beyond the Go standard library.

Assumptions & figures used

The exact thresholds, ceilings and fees — all indicative

Each of these is a single named constant in the source, is stated here, and can be changed inside the tool if the authority revises it.

  • Petty food business operator turnover ceiling: ₹12,00,000 a year.
  • Turnover above which a Central Licence is needed: ₹20,00,00,000 a year.
  • Petty food business operator food capacity: 100 kg a day.
  • Food capacity a State Licence covers: 2 metric tonnes a day.
  • Petty food business operator milk ceiling: 500 litres a day.
  • Milk a State Licence covers: 50,000 litres a day.
  • Milk solids a State Licence covers: 2,500 metric tonnes a year.
  • States of operation that trigger a Central Licence: 2.
  • Basic Registration fee: ₹100 a year.
  • State Licence fee: ₹2,000 a year for food service and for any other food business; ₹3,000 a year for a manufacturing or processing unit at or below 1 metric tonne a day of food and 10,000 litres a day of milk; ₹5,000 a year above that band.
  • Central Licence fee: ₹7,500 a year, one flat rate.
  • Validity: 1 to 5 years, your choice at application; the fee is per year.
  • Renewal alerts: 90, 30 and 7 days before expiry. A licence counts as valid through the whole of its expiry date.
  • Indicative late renewal penalty: ₹100 a day after expiry.

Two deliberate simplifications. Production capacity and milk volumes are read as declared, so traders, retailers and restaurants should leave them at zero. The milk solids figure only decides the step up to a Central Licence, because the petty ceiling is written in litres a day.

These figures are indicative and they change. FSSAI revises fees, turnover thresholds and capacity ceilings from time to time, and the document list varies by state, by food category and by the kind of entity you are. This tool is a planning aid — it is not legal advice, it is not a filing service, and it does not submit anything to FSSAI on your behalf. Confirm your eligibility, the current fee and the required documents on the official FoSCoS portal, or with your state's Food Safety Commissionerate, before you apply or renew.
